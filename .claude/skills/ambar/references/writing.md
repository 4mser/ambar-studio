# Writing

Everything a person reads or hears: narration, titles, callouts, slide text, document copy.

## Voice

- Write like a person who knows the subject and respects the reader's time. Short sentences. Concrete nouns and verbs.
- Say what something does, with the real names of things: the button label, the report name, the number.
- One claim per sentence. If it needs a number, give the number and where it came from.
- Use the language in `AMBAR.md`. In Spanish: neutral Spanish, "tú" (never "vos"), no Anglicisms when a common Spanish word exists.

## Avoid

- Hype and filler: "revolutionary", "seamless", "cutting-edge", "unlock", "supercharge", "game-changer", "effortless", "in today's fast-paced world", "Let's dive in", "Imagine a world".
- The "it's not X, it's Y" construction and its variants ("more than a tool, a…"; "no es X, es Y").
- Rhetorical questions as openers, exclamation marks, triplets added for rhythm, em dashes as a tic.
- Restating the narration on screen. The screen shows the key word or number; the voice says the sentence.
- Inventing facts: features, numbers, customers, quotes, dates. If the brief does not give it and the source does not show it, leave it out or ask.

## Narration

- Read each line aloud in your head. If it does not sound like something a person would say, rewrite it.
- Spell out what TTS mispronounces: "AMBAR" not "ÁMBAR" for `say` voices in English, numbers as words when the voice stumbles, no symbols ("percent", not "%").
- Leave pauses with `pause` in `ambar.json`, not with punctuation tricks.

## On screen

- Headlines up to 7 words. Callouts 2 to 5 words. Labels 1 to 3.
- Sentence case. Highlight one word, not three.
